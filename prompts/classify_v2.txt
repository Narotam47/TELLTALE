CORE RULE

Classify by the FUNCTION THE ROLE ACTUALLY PERFORMS, not by the team or department
name attached to it, and not by a single keyword in the title. Department labels are
inconsistent across companies: one company's "Business Technology" team may hold
platform engineers, another's may hold operations analysts. Read the title and
description and decide what the person in this seat would spend their day doing.

PAYMENTS INFRASTRUCTURE vs ENGINEERING - PLATFORM

At a payments company, an engineering role is Payments Infrastructure when the system
it builds is payments-specific: settlement, reconciliation engines, UPI, card rails,
switches, gateways, ledgers, or PSP/bank integrations. It is NOT Engineering - Platform.

Engineering - Platform is for infrastructure that is NOT payments-specific: CI/CD,
cloud and Kubernetes, observability, SRE, databases, internal developer tooling, and
data platform work. The same job title can land in either category depending on the
system named in the description.

  "Engineering Manager - Financial Services" (description covers building the
  settlement and bank-integration stack) -> Payments Infrastructure. The managed
  system is payments rails, so the payments-specific rule wins over the generic
  backend reading.

  "Site Reliability Engineer - Big Data" -> Engineering - Platform. Reliability and
  big-data infrastructure are not payments-specific, even at a payments company.

  "MySQL - Senior Lead Database Engineer" -> Engineering - Platform. Database
  engineering is generic infrastructure; nothing here touches payment rails.

A payments keyword in the title does NOT by itself mean Payments Infrastructure. The
role must BUILD or OPERATE the rails:

  "Product Management - Product Manager - UPI" -> Product. Owns a UPI product roadmap;
  it is a product seat that happens to sit on a payments surface.

  "Growth Management - Senior Manager - User Growth (UPI)" -> Growth & Marketing.
  Drives UPI user acquisition, not the UPI system itself.

  "Reconciliation Specialist" -> Finance & Accounting. Performs reconciliation as a
  finance process; it does not build the reconciliation engine.

GROWTH & MARKETING vs PRODUCT vs OPERATIONS

Growth & Marketing: acquiring, activating or monetising users and merchants — sales,
business development, partnerships, account management, campaigns, brand, ad sales.

Product: owning what gets built — roadmap, requirements, prioritisation, specs.

Operations: keeping an existing process running day to day — support, back-office
processing, logistics, facilities, field operations, service delivery.

  "Govt. Ad Sales - Sr. Manager/DGM - Paytm Ads" -> Growth & Marketing. Selling ad
  inventory is revenue generation, not process operation.

  "Customer Support Agent - Executive - Bill Payments" -> Operations. Front-line
  support resolving user issues on a running service.

  "Associate, Startup Accounts" -> Growth & Marketing. Account management for a
  customer segment is a go-to-market seat despite the generic Associate title.

  "Facility Manager" -> Operations. Manages workplace facilities; "Manager" here
  denotes a process owner, not a product or growth function.

Collections and recovery roles are Credit & Risk, NOT Operations, even when the title
reads like an operations job:

  "Cluster Collections Manager - Nashik" -> Credit & Risk. Recovering delinquent
  loans is a credit-risk function regardless of the regional-manager framing.

"ANALYST" DETERMINES NOTHING

The word "Analyst" in a title carries no categorical information. Classify by WHAT the
analyst analyses:

  "Growth/Business Analyst - Recharges & Utilities" -> Growth & Marketing. Analyses
  growth metrics for a business line.

  "Fraud Analyst" -> Credit & Risk. Analyses fraud and risk exposure.

  "Financial Analyst" -> Finance & Accounting. Analyses company financials.

Data & ML requires the role to BUILD models, pipelines, or data systems — not to
consume dashboards and reports. A business analyst reading reports is not Data & ML;
a data engineer building the warehouse is.

  "Data Engineering - Senior / Lead Engineer" -> Data & ML. Builds data pipelines.

SENIORITY

Infer from title first: Intern; Associate/Analyst/Executive/Trainee -> Junior;
unqualified IC titles -> Mid; "Senior" -> Senior; "Staff"/"Principal"/"Architect" ->
Staff/Principal; "Head"/"Director"/"VP"/"Chief"/"General Manager" -> Leadership.

A "Manager" or "Team Leader" title that manages a PROCESS rather than PEOPLE is Mid or
Senior, not Leadership. Reserve Leadership for roles owning an org, a function, or
multiple teams. An "Engineering Manager" of a single team is Senior, not Leadership.
Use years-of-experience in the description to break ties; when the description gives
no signal, prefer the more conservative (lower) level rather than guessing upward.

RATIONALE MUST STAND ALONE

Each rationale is stored and read on its own, detached from this batch. Every rationale
must be independently intelligible and must name the evidence it used. NEVER refer to
another posting in the batch. Phrases like "same as above", "as with the previous
role", "similar to the earlier posting", or "ditto" are FORBIDDEN — a rationale
containing one is useless and counts as a failed classification. Repeat the reasoning
in full for every posting, even when two postings are near-identical.

AMBIGUITY

If a posting genuinely fits no category above, use Other and say explicitly in the
rationale WHY it was ambiguous. Do not stretch a poor-fitting category to avoid Other,
and do not reach for Other when a real category fits.
